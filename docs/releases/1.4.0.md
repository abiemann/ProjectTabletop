# Project Tabletop 1.4.0

Duck Hunt joins the tabletop with three modes: a mechanical saloon shooting gallery, flying mallards and clay pigeon pairs. Aim with a red dot and use a locally learned click sound to shoot.

## Duck Hunt

- **Target Practice:** two independent saloon rows reveal five crystal skulls and five paper targets on a conveyor. Skulls shatter into settling glass and show yellow eye feedback while aimed at; paper retains each puncture. Missing into an exposed row's backdrop closes that row. A subsequent middle stage presents five moving paper targets, each facing the player for up to three seconds or half a second after its first hit. A golden countdown arc shows the remaining window.
- **Scoring:** skulls award 100 points. Each paper target's first hit awards 100 inside its inner ring, 50 between the rings, or zero outside the outer ring. A mechanical split-flap total and floating golden score text show awards. Animated lamps, engraved covers, Western round messages and a golden ceiling complete the gallery.
- **HUNT:** ten mallards launch one at a time from varied positions, becoming faster throughout the round. Articulated wings preserve the visible hit silhouette; a hit freezes and blinks the photographed pose and awards 100 points. The next round resets speed and score.
- **Clay Pigeon:** ten pairs launch automatically after PLAY, with half a second between the two clays. They bank and recede over a valley, shatter into textured ceramic fragments when hit, and award 100 points each. Small horizon flocks are decorative scenery.
- **Controls and artwork:** each mode opens its standard EXIT/BACK/PLAY drawer and closes it when play begins. HUNT and Clay Pigeon display PRESS PLAY before starting; Clay Pigeon shows GAME OVER and PLAY AGAIN afterward. Improved foreground grass in both outdoor scenes preserves the original sharp sky, trees and distant landscape. Backgrounds remain native 1672 × 941 images, not native 4K.

## Aiming, sound and shared controls

Gun-click Learning guides quiet-room sampling, ten learning clicks, five validation clicks and a final quiet check before explicit saving. Profiles contain numerical sound features for the selected microphone; routine learning and gameplay do not retain raw audio. Missing selected microphones are reported instead of silently switching inputs.

Shots use fresh camera observations within the same click interval. Shared optical improvements reject more scenery, lettering and metallic-reflection lookalikes while retaining genuine red and magenta laser responses. Missing or independently ambiguous dots cannot become a hit from an old aim. Moving-target checks use the total projector-to-camera delay measured during board setup to reconstruct the scene visible in the selected photo.

Menu paging and arrow-hold fixes prevent intact lettering from leaving navigation blocked after an app restart. The Duck Hunt menu preview uses a proportional scenery crop. The laptop's projection preview now starts off each session; enabling it does not change projector output or input tracking. Recording & diagnostics also starts off each session and must be enabled to retain automatic tester logs and camera clips.

The laptop's **Estimated Visual Latency** uses the total delay measured during board setup, minus a one-refresh display allowance derived from the selected projector's active refresh rate. No manual entry is needed. This is an estimate of camera exposure, buffering and delivery together, not a measurement of the projector's full processing delay. Moving-target hit reconstruction uses the measured total delay, preserving the camera timestamp's original freshness checks.

## Download and install

Download **ProjectTabletop-1.4.0-win-x64-setup.exe** from the release assets. It targets Windows 11 x64 and includes .NET, the Windows App SDK, native dependencies, hand models, artwork, fonts and sounds. Setup installs for the current account without administrator rights. Updates preserve settings, saved games, learned profiles and exported pictures.

The installer is unsigned. `SHA256SUMS.txt` records the downloadable asset hashes; the JSON payload manifest identifies the source commit and packaged files. Source archives are separate from the Windows setup. See the [installation guide](https://github.com/abiemann/ProjectTabletop/blob/v1.4.0/docs/INSTALL.md) for microphone learning and the three modes.

## Validation and limits

The final outdoor artwork and rendering passed **740 HUNT and 329 Clay Pigeon native assertions**, including multiple display aspects, clear-scene optical checks, simulated laser hits and game-state rendering. Pixel comparisons verified that protected original scenery remained unchanged. Earlier full-suite and native-control milestones, captured-camera/audio replays and development-tabletop observations are recorded separately in the [README](https://github.com/abiemann/ProjectTabletop/blob/v1.4.0/README.md#duck-hunt); their counts and live results belong to the changes tested at those milestones.

The Windows release workflow builds and runs the offline suite, verifies the payload, and checks installation, startup, board navigation, GPU preview rendering, shutdown and uninstall before publication. These checks do not replace physical camera/projector acceptance. Earlier live rounds confirmed improved shot response on the development tabletop, but do not establish a statistical long-term hit rate. Frame timing, sound recognition and optical ambiguity still depend on the camera, microphone, projector and room.

Sustained camera capture stalls remain unresolved on the development hardware. A stalled or disconnected stream pauses input and requires an explicit Stop/Start camera followed by board setup. Broad hardware acceptance, sustained presentation measurements and several previously documented board-specific physical checks remain outstanding. Real-card recognition and depth-based touch are outside this release's scope.
