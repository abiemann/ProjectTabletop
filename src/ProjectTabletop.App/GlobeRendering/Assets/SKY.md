# Globe sky imagery

`milky-way-sky-0.png` through `milky-way-sky-3.png` contain the full celestial sphere from [NASA Scientific Visualization Studio's Deep Star Maps 2020](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851/). The source maps measured star positions, brightness and colours from astronomical catalogues. It is a scientific sky visualization, not a single telescope photograph or generated illustration. Earth and cloud sources are documented separately in the app's third-party notices.

Credit: **NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio. Gaia DR2: ESA/Gaia/DPAC.**

NASA imagery is used under its [media usage guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/). See also the [Gaia DR2 credit and citation instructions](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR2/Miscellaneous/sec_credit_and_citation_instructions/). ## Source and preparation

- Original: [starmap_2020_16k_gal.exr](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004800/a004851/starmap_2020_16k_gal.exr), 16384 × 8192, linear HDR, galactic coordinates in plate carrée projection.
- Original SHA-256: `20363595f309da9626111937d6d75555530937914527abbd6d81132764434aa7`.
- Bundled output: four opaque RGB PNG longitude tiles, each 4096 × 8192, sRGB. Together they preserve all 16384 × 8192 source texels without resizing. Tile 0 contains columns 0–4095, tile 1 columns 4096–8191, tile 2 columns 8192–12287 and tile 3 columns 12288–16383.
- Preparation script: `tools/prepare_globe_nasa_sky.py` in the source repository. NumPy, OpenEXR and Pillow are preparation dependencies only; the app does not need them.

With `--panorama`, the script verifies the original checksum and dimensions, multiplies each linear-light texel by exposure 4, clips to the display range and encodes standard sRGB. It splits the result into four lossless longitude tiles; each file remains below 100 MiB and within an 8192-pixel texture dimension. The tiles together occupy approximately 512 MiB when decoded as RGBA8 on the GPU. No synthetic stars, clouds, glow, resizing or generative edits are added. Per-tile bounds, file sizes and checksums are also recorded in the preparation JSON.

The original EXR and an equivalent full-panorama PNG remain preparation artifacts and are not bundled. The full PNG is 177857434 bytes with SHA-256 `e55bab898f7399d7f1ac5ba44390e2438379d5cdc602ebf533eeee3158e7b738`; splitting it into tiles preserves every decoded RGB pixel.

Without `--panorama`, the script retains the earlier static comparison mode: a 4096 × 4096 stereographic view centred at galactic longitude 0°, latitude 0°, with a 160° field along each axis and 35° roll. It averages four subpixel samples in linear light before exposure and sRGB encoding. That earlier static PNG has SHA-256 `b7f687880c13fbdbfd7bbaa38b3f699de810a18728926104657716f89d1f296d` and is retained only as a comparison artifact.

## Runtime presentation

The view looks outward from inside the celestial sphere. The opening uses the same stereographic projection, galactic centre and 35° roll as the approved static view. The 160° square field is cropped to the physical board aspect, giving approximately 160° × 101.07° on a 16:9 board. Direction vectors rotate in three dimensions at 0.03° per second, or one full turn in three hours and twenty minutes, before sampling the galactic panorama. This reveals new parts of the sky rather than spinning a flat bitmap. Runtime sampling joins adjacent tile edges and wraps the longitude seam. The shader averages four quarter-pixel samples from the sRGB textures; unlike the earlier offline static preparation, this is not HDR linear-light averaging.

The NASA background displays at its prepared brightness across the whole board. No runtime dimming, regional brightness mask or transition dithering is applied. Earth and its blue atmosphere are sampled at four quarter-pixel offsets through the surface and visible shell, paired with the existing sky samples. The original scattering hue, strength and falloff are preserved. Beyond 1.15 planet radii plus edge coverage, the original faint atmospheric tail uses one sample. This avoids a full-screen blur, extra render target or multiplying sky texture lookups. Comets and controls retain their own shading.

Occasional decorative comets retain their longer paths and tails: travel is 70% farther at the earlier velocity, lasting 1.36–2.04 seconds, with tails 30% longer. Their thin warm-white/blue-gold trail remains behind Earth and above the bottom-quarter swipe area. These effects are separate from NASA's star data.
